# Legends Split on AI, Converge on Caution

Put the playbooks of sixteen famous investors against the market of November 10, 2026, and most of them reach the same verdict. US stocks are expensive on long-run measures, long-dated Treasuries are risky, and the AI boom is either a bubble or close to one. Very few of them would go fully to cash or bet against the whole market. The S&P 500 is about **7,550**, up roughly 7–10% for the year. On next year's expected earnings it looks cheap at **17.7x**, its lowest in more than three years, because earnings have grown **more than 25%** for three straight quarters. On long-run measures it looks extreme: the Shiller CAPE (price against ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings) is **about 39–41**, and total market value against GDP is **about 220–260%** ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-november-6-2026); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/56/sp-500-shiller-cape-ratio); [USMacro](https://usmacro.com/indicator/buffett_indicator/)). The backdrop is unusual. A Fed under Kevin Warsh **raised rates in September** and is about 85–88% priced to raise again on December 9. Nvidia is **about 30% below its October 6 record**. Gold is **22–28% below its January peak**. A US–Iran war has whipsawed oil ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260916.pdf); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/october-cpi-report-elevated-inflation/); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/nvidia-stock-pe-ratio-lowest-since-2019)). Berkshire Hathaway shows the dominant response in miniature: it holds **$377B** in cash and T-bills while buying back a record **$9.6B** of its own stock in Q3 and buying whole businesses such as Taylor Morrison ([Insurance Journal/Bloomberg](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/11/09/891933.htm)). The real split is over AI. Michael Burry, Ray Dalio and Stanley Druckenmiller are positioned for it to unwind. Cathie Wood is buying the dip. Bill Ackman and Seth Klarman are buying AI-adjacent "quality" names only at prices they can defend. Paul Tudor Jones says ride it with stops and brace for 2027. For an ordinary investor, the lesson the legends share is not a stock tip. It is a process: own a low-cost diversified core, insist on a margin of safety (a buffer between price and value), treat cash yielding about 4% as an option to buy later rather than a failure, and stay away from leverage and gambling.

> **How to read this report.** For each investor, the report separates three things: (1) strategy and rules, (2) what they or their firm actually did in 2025–2026, where known, and (3) a prediction of their view and likely trades today, **explicitly labeled "Inference."** Two data limits matter. First, the research behind this report could not open most source pages directly, so most 2025–2026 figures come from **search-result summaries** of the cited articles, not full texts. Second, holdings data comes from **Q2 2026 13F filings**, which show positions as of June 30, 2026, and so are **up to about 4.5 months old**. Q3 2026 13Fs are due around November 14–16, 2026, and had not been filed as of November 10. A 13F also shows only US-listed long stocks and options. It never shows short sales, currency trades, bonds or cash.

## The market these legends face mixes 1999 valuations with 1970s macro

The core tension in November 2026 is that **profits have run ahead of prices** and still have not caught up with long-run valuation history. FactSet's forward 12-month EPS estimate is **up 33.6% year to date while the index is up only 7.4%** (as of November 5), and 84% of companies beat Q3 estimates ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-november-6-2026)). That is why the forward P/E is low (17.7x) while CAPE sits near dot-com levels. Our own calculation shows how thin the cushion is. The forward earnings yield (about 5.65%) is only **about 0.8 percentage points above the 4.88% ten-year Treasury yield**, and the CAPE earnings yield (about 2.6%) is **about 2.3 points below it** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)). The market is also extremely concentrated. The Magnificent 7 are **34.6% of the S&P 500** and the top ten are about 38%. At the 2000 peak the top ten were about 27% ([HistoryOfMarket](https://historyofmarket.com/articles/magnificent-7-sp500-weight); [ChartRow](https://chartrow.com/sp500/concentration)).

The macro backdrop rewards patience more than it did in 2025. Q3 GDP grew **3.4%**, October payrolls rose **165,000**, and October core CPI rose **0.3% for a third straight month**. Governor Waller had said a 50-basis-point hike should be considered if that happened ([BEA](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/jobs-report-october-2026.html); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/october-2026-cpi-inflation-report)). The fiscal 2026 deficit is about **$2.0–2.1T**. That got worse after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs and roughly $100B was refunded ([CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62704)). The AI trade has moved into a correction even though the businesses are still growing fast. Nvidia's latest quarter brought in **$96.2B of revenue, up 106%**, yet the stock trades at about **20x forward earnings, its lowest since 2019**. Investors worry about circular financing among Nvidia, OpenAI, Oracle and CoreWeave, about hyperscaler free cash flow turning negative, and about a newly Democratic Congress restricting data-center construction ([NVIDIA IR](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/11/62956559/nvidia-stock-selloff)). Outside AI, public credit spreads look calm, with high-yield at about **342 bp**. Stress is building underneath: CCC-rated debt is at about **1,320 bp**, and Fitch reports a **record 6.3% private-credit default rate** ([MacroRadar](https://www.macroradar.io/high-yield-spread); [Coutts](https://www.coutts.com/insights/investing/is-private-credit-under-pressure.html)). Abroad, **Japan's Nikkei is up 25% YTD**, EM is up 13.8%, and China's CSI 300 entered a **bear market** in October ([ETFdb](https://etfdb.com/china-insights-content-hub/world-markets-watchlist-november-2-2026/); [Janus Henderson](https://www.janushenderson.com/social-retail/article/market-moves-themes-that-mattered-october-2026/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). Sentiment is split. Retail investors are fearful (AAII bears at **48.4%**), while institutions stay heavily in tech, with BofA survey cash at **3.9%**, a level the survey treats as a sell signal ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/sentimentsurvey); [Atrani Capital](https://atranicapital.substack.com/p/october-2026-bank-of-america-global-fund-manager-survey)).

## Berkshire under Abel is deploying cash without abandoning Buffett's caution

### Warren Buffett

**Strategy and rules.** Buffett began as a Graham-style "cigar-butt" bargain hunter: buy a poor business cheaply enough to profit. Under Munger's influence he moved to "a wonderful company at a fair price," meaning businesses with durable competitive advantages (moats) that he holds for decades ([1989 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html)). The rules that never change are a margin of safety, staying inside his circle of competence, almost no leverage, contempt for market timing, and keeping a large T-bill reserve to use when others panic ("be fearful when others are greedy," [NYT, 2008](https://www.nytimes.com/2008/10/17/opinion/17buffett.html)). He calls gold unproductive ([2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)) and derivatives "financial weapons of mass destruction" ([2002 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2002pdf.pdf)). For ordinary investors he recommends **90% in a low-cost S&P 500 index fund and 10% in short-term government bonds** ([2013 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2013ltr.pdf)). He called market cap to GNP "probably the best single measure" of valuation ([Fortune, 2001](https://money.cnn.com/magazines/fortune/fortune_archive/2001/12/10/314691/index.htm)).

**What Berkshire is doing in 2025–2026.** Buffett stepped down as CEO on December 31, 2025. He remains chairman and said he is "going quiet," while Greg Abel became CEO ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/warren-buffett-pens-last-letter-abel-prepares-take-over)). Under Buffett, Berkshire was a net seller of stocks for about 13 straight quarters. It cut Apple from more than 900M shares to about 228M, began selling Bank of America in July 2024, and built cash to a **record $381.7B in Q3 2025** ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/11/06/warren-buffett-keeps-selling-stocks-builds-record-381-billion-cash-stash-is-a-market-crash-imminent/)). In 2026, Abel's Berkshire started spending. It closed the **$9.5B OxyChem** deal on January 2 ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000119312526083899/brka-20251231.htm)) and bought homebuilder **Taylor Morrison for about $6.8B** in equity, closing in July ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/berkshire-hathaway-completes-acquisition-of-taylor-morrison-302834141.html)). Buybacks went from zero (May 2024–February 2026) to **$0.23B in Q1, $4.5B in Q2 and $9.6B in Q3**, at an average of about $507 per B share in September ([GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/11/07/berkshire-hathaway-accelerates-stock-buybacks-operating-profit-falls/); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/09/berkshire-kept-buying-back-stock-as-shares-hit-records-heres-what-the-price-it-paid-tells-you/)). Abel's first 13F (Q1 2026) exited 16 positions, including Visa, Mastercard, Amazon and UnitedHealth. It opened stakes in **Delta** (partly reversing Buffett's 2020 airline exit) and Macy's ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/berkshire-ceo-greg-abel-sells-144136494.html)). By Q2 2026, **Alphabet had grown to about 106M shares (about $37.8B)**, the third-largest holding ([ECM Source](https://ecmsource.com/berkshire-hathaway-q2-2026-13f-alphabet-83-percent-august-2026/)). The Japanese trading houses are about $46B and are considered "50 years or forever" holdings ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/09/warren-buffett-successor-greg-abel-46-billion-brka/)). In Q3 2026, Berkshire was again a small **net seller of stocks** (bought about $2.4B, sold about $2.8B). Cash stood at **$377.4B** on the headline measure, or about $352–357B adjusted ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/07/berkshire-hathaway-stock-brk-q3-earnings-investment-portfolio-buybacks/); [Berkshire 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001067983/000119312526452530/brka-ex99_1.htm)). In May 2026, Buffett called markets "a church with a casino attached" ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/world/us/story/church-with-a-casino-attached-buffett-sounds-alarm-on-rise-of-market-gambling-529062-2026-05-04)). *Data caveat: which stocks Berkshire bought and sold in Q3 will not be known until the 13F due around November 16.*

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** With market cap to GDP at roughly 220–260%, Buffett's own preferred gauge reads at or near a record. On his framework the broad market is fully priced, and the casino comment shows he sees speculation as excessive. His playbook does not call for shorting or predicting a top. It calls for doing four things at once. First, keep a very large reserve in T-bills, now yielding about 4% given a fed funds range of 3.75–4.00%. Second, keep trimming richly valued holdings. Third, buy back BRK whenever it trades below intrinsic value. Fourth, buy whole real-economy businesses or add to franchises on drawdowns. The AI selloff has probably pushed the Alphabet stake below its recent marks. Buffett's habit of "buying the temporary problem," seen in American Express and Coca-Cola, suggests Berkshire is more likely to hold or add Alphabet than to sell it. More Japan exposure funded with yen debt, more housing and industrial assets, and continued trimming of Bank of America and Apple are also consistent with his past behavior. He would not touch gold, Bitcoin or leveraged AI-infrastructure credit.

### Charlie Munger

**Strategy and rules.** Munger, who died on November 28, 2023, moved Buffett toward quality: pay up for pricing power and low capital needs, as with See's Candies ([2007 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf)). His toolkit was concentration in a few great businesses, inversion ("invert, always invert," meaning ask what would cause failure), a "latticework" of mental models drawn from many fields, and a catalog of human misjudgment. He said he would never sell Costco even at about 40x earnings, which shows he would pay for quality while staying aware of price ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/1952521/)). He called crypto "rat poison" and a "venereal disease" ([Investing.com/Reuters](https://investing.com/news/stock-market-news/buffett-business-partner-munger-laments-uschina-tensions-calls-crypto-venereal-disease-2765779)).

**What his legacy is doing.** His signature idea, BYD, bought in 2008 for $230M, returned more than **20x** before Berkshire fully exited in 2025 ([CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2025/09/22/buffets-berkshire-completely-exits-byd-selling-all-remaining-shares-as-chinese-automaker-face-slow-down)). Abel said at the 2026 meeting that no single person replaces Munger as top adviser ([Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/antitrust/berkshire-meeting-highlights-tough-balancing-act-for-greg-abel)). In 2023, Munger called Alibaba "one of the worst mistakes I ever made" yet stayed optimistic on China ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://news.yahoo.com/charlie-munger-downplays-risk-taiwan-215840188.html)).

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Inverting the AI boom, Munger would ask what turns asset-light compounders into utilities. His answer would be capex that consumes most of operating cash flow: Alphabet's Q3 capex was reportedly 121% of its operating cash flow ([Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/hyperscaler-capex-q3-2026)). He would be skeptical of circular vendor financing and contemptuous of the options-driven "casino." He would sit tight on a handful of franchises with pricing power and buy only on clear mispricing. China's CSI 300 bear market is the kind of setup he historically found tempting for a few high-quality Chinese franchises. After the Alibaba lesson he would demand a much bigger margin of safety, so treat this as a low-confidence inference. He would buy no crypto.

## Value and fundamental investors split between caution and quality bargains

### Benjamin Graham

**Strategy and rules.** Graham's system is quantitative and anchored in the balance sheet. Treat "Mr. Market" (the market personified as a moody partner who quotes a new price every day) as a counterparty to exploit, not a guide. Buy only with a margin of safety. Keep a defensive portfolio **between 25% and 75% in stocks**, leaning toward bonds when stocks are expensive ([Intelligent Investor summary](https://intelligentinvestor-internetbusiness.blogspot.com/2009/11/general-portfolio-policy-defensive.html)). Defensive buyers want a P/E of 15 or less, a price-to-book of 1.5 or less, and long dividend records (the "Graham Number" multiplies these into one ceiling price). Enterprising investors hunt **net-nets**, stocks priced below net current assets minus all liabilities ([Wikipedia: Graham number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_number); [Graham's Two-Investor Framework](https://glenbradfordcom.vercel.app/defensive-vs-enterprising-investor)).

**What is happening now.** Graham died in 1976, so there is no current portfolio. His metrics read extreme today: CAPE near 40, compared with a long-run average near 17 ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/56/sp-500-shiller-cape-ratio)).

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Graham compared stock earnings yields with high-grade bond yields. A CAPE earnings yield of about 2.6% against a 4.88% ten-year Treasury yield is a clear signal to move toward the bond end of his range. A defensive Graham follower would sit at about **25–40% equities**, with the rest in high-grade Treasuries and T-bills. His screens would exclude nearly all AI mega-caps. An enterprising follower would fish for low price-to-book insurers, financials, industrial cyclicals, small caps and Japanese stocks. Graham would label the AI-momentum trade speculation, acceptable only in a small, ring-fenced account.

### Peter Lynch

**Strategy and rules.** At Magellan, Lynch earned about **29.2% a year from 1977 to 1990**, nearly double the S&P 500 ([Validea](https://blog.validea.com/?p=13923)). His method sorts every stock into one of six categories: slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, asset plays and turnarounds. He prices growth with the **PEG ratio** (P/E divided by the growth rate, where about 1.0 is fair) and looks for "tenbaggers" (stocks that rise tenfold) among businesses he understands ([Validea: Lynch approach](https://blog.validea.com/?p=7627)).

**What he is doing in 2025–2026.** Lynch has no public 13F. In October 2025 he said he owns **no AI stocks**, "literally couldn't pronounce Nvidia until about 8 months ago," and had "no idea" whether AI had gone too far ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/06/peter-lynch-ai-trade-investing.html)). He noted that Costco carried an "AI stock multiple" despite selling paper towels ([Compound with Rene](https://www.compoundwithrene.com/p/free-7-timeless-takeaways-from-peter)). No 2026 interview was found.

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Lynch's framework fits this market better than most: an index P/E of 17.7 on earnings growth above 25% implies a market-level PEG well below 1. He would warn, though, that much of that growth is cyclical. Semiconductors belong in his "cyclical" bucket, and buying a cyclical at a low P/E near peak earnings is the classic trap he warned about. Nvidia at 20x after a 106% revenue jump fits that description. Energy profits, inflated by $90–118 oil, are another example. He would stay out of AI names he cannot explain. He would avoid "AI-multiple" stalwarts like Costco on price. He would look instead for understandable mid-cap growers at PEGs near 1, consumer turnarounds, and asset plays such as housing, which is the theme Berkshire chose with Taylor Morrison.

### Philip Fisher

**Strategy and rules.** Fisher's *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits* (1958) set out "scuttlebutt": field research gathered from a company's customers, suppliers, competitors and former employees. He judged companies against a **15-point checklist** covering long-run sales growth, R&D effectiveness, profit margins, depth of management and, non-negotiably, management "of unquestionable integrity" ([Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/philip-fishers-15-points/)). On selling he said the time to sell is "almost never." Valid reasons are a mistake in the original analysis or a deteriorating business, never a market forecast ([moomoo](https://www.moomoo.com/us/learn/the-investment-strategy-of-philip-fisher)). He held Motorola from 1955 until his death in 2004, and John Train famously described Buffett as "85% Graham, 15% Fisher" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Arthur_Fisher)).

**What is happening now.** He has no current portfolio, and no audited track record exists.

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Fisher would ignore the bubble debate and the Fed entirely. He would run scuttlebutt across the AI supply chain to find the few firms with proven margins and R&D leadership. Nvidia's 75% gross margin and its position with customers would interest him. He would then concentrate and hold through drawdowns like the current 30% decline. He differs from Wood in demanding proven margins and candid management rather than projections of total addressable market (TAM). He differs from Bogle in rejecting diversification for its own sake. Hyperscalers' rising debt and dilution risk would count against them on his checklist.

### Howard Marks

**Strategy and rules.** Marks's framework includes "second-level thinking" (asking what is already priced in), risk defined as the chance of permanent loss rather than volatility, knowing where we stand in the cycle, and a psychological pendulum that swings between greed and fear. His memos apply it. In "Is It a Bubble?" (December 2025) he classified AI as an **"inflection bubble"**: a transformative technology that attracts too much capital, unlike the mean-reverting subprime bubble. He flagged circular deals, debt piling into a technologically uncertain sector, and a possible data-center glut ([Oaktree](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/is-it-a-bubble)).

**What he is doing in 2025–2026.** His February 2026 memo "AI Hurtles Ahead" recommended "a moderate position, applied with selectivity and prudence" ([Oaktree](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/ai-hurtles-ahead)). In April 2026 he warned that private-credit direct lending faces its **first major test since the financial crisis** ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4573849-howard-marks-outlines-growing-concerns-about-private-credit-in-new-memo)). His September 22, 2026 memo argued that deficits plus AI investment are creating heavy demand for capital that pressures long-dated government yields ([Oaktree](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/shall-we-repeal-the-laws-of-economics---part-iii)). On November 2, 2026, he said "there is no reward in the investment business for hyperactivity" ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/04/billionaire-howard-marks-has-a-warning-for-ai-investors-theres-no-reward-for-rushing-in)).

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Marks would place the pendulum at "elevated, not euphoric everywhere." His sharpest observation would be the gap inside credit. High-yield at about 342 bp pays little for risk. CCCs at 1,320 bp and a 6.3% private-credit default rate are the cracks that come before distressed opportunities. He would keep only modest, selective equity exposure. He would favor senior secured and opportunistic credit over broad high-yield, and short duration over long Treasuries. He would hold dry powder for forced selling in over-levered AI-infrastructure debt (CoreWeave notes reportedly yielded about 15% in October) and in software-heavy direct-lending books ([Better than Random](https://betterthanrandom.substack.com/p/the-ai-bubble-is-now-running-on-debt)).

### Seth Klarman

**Strategy and rules.** Klarman's *Margin of Safety* (1991) puts avoiding permanent loss first and targets absolute rather than relative returns. It treats value as a range and finds bargains among forced sellers, complex situations and bankruptcies. Holding cash is legitimate when nothing meets the bar, but in 2026 he said "I'm not getting paid to hold cash, I'm getting paid to use my judgment" ([iConnections](https://iconnections.io/insights/seth-klarman-baupost-ai-era-global-alts-ny-2026/)).

**What Baupost is doing in 2025–2026.** In 2026, Klarman said the market has **"characteristics of a bubble."** Baupost neither hides in cash nor chases AI. It holds about **10% in AI beneficiaries at multiples he can defend**, owns raw land near power infrastructure as optionality, and is buying software-company credits from AI "losers" at very low cash-flow multiples ([iConnections](https://iconnections.io/insights/seth-klarman-baupost-ai-era-global-alts-ny-2026/)). In June he argued that the big names carrying a concentrated market are often expensive ([Ritholtz](https://ritholtz.com/2026/06/transcript-seth-klarman/)). The **Q2 2026 13F** shows about $5.42B across 23 names. It is led by **Amazon (about 16.5%)**, Elevance Health and Restaurant Brands, with new stakes in **CME Group and Pershing Square** and additions to Amazon, Alphabet and Ferguson ([Investingly](https://investingly.io/investors/baupost-group); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/14/seth-klarman-just-quietly-bought-a-piece-of-bill-ackmans-firm/)). *These holdings are as of June 30 and cover only part of Baupost's assets. Private, credit and real-estate positions do not appear.*

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Klarman is likely a patient buyer in the AI unwind, not a short seller. The best fits are distressed or stressed AI-infrastructure and software debt, now that CCC and private-credit stress are rising, and quality compounders after drawdowns, such as Amazon and Alphabet. Healthcare value after the managed-care selloff fits too. He would stay away from crowded mega-cap AI beta.

### Michael Burry

**Strategy and rules.** Burry is a deep-value contrarian who concentrates heavily and expresses big-picture views through options. He made his name with subprime credit default swaps (CDS) in 2005–07 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Burry)).

**What he is doing in 2025–2026.** His Q3 2025 13F revealed puts with about **$1.1B notional value on Palantir and Nvidia** ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/michael-burry-buys-puts-on-nvidia-palantir-4330249)). He accused hyperscalers of understating depreciation by about **$176B over 2026–2028** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/11/big-short-investor-michael-burry-accuses-ai-hyperscalers-of-artificially-boosting-earnings.html)). He then deregistered Scion and now discloses trades through a paid Substack, so he files no more 13Fs ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/13/michael-burry-of-big-short-fame-deregisters-scion-asset-management.html)). Through 2026 he added shorts on Tesla, Micron, Caterpillar, Oracle and Nebius and warned of a possible "1987-type fall" ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/12/michael-burry-is-staying-short-nvidia-palantir-and/)). In October he consolidated into **QQQ puts expiring around April 2027 and SMH puts expiring around June 2027**, predicted a market top within **0–9 months**, and raised his MercadoLibre long by 50% ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/michael-burry-shifts-bearish-bets-to-nasdaq-semiconductor-etf-puts-sees-market-top-within-9-months/cZxIPRTRBF8)). On **November 5, 2026**, as SMH fell about 11%, he **sold 25% of his SMH puts** and said he remains "very bearish" ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/06/michael-burry-sold-25-of-his-chip-puts-and-says-hes-still-very-bearish/)). *Reporting on his trades is secondary and sometimes contradicts itself on dates and instruments.*

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Burry would see the 30% Nvidia drop, OpenAI's reportedly lower revenue run rate, and CoreWeave's distressed bonds as early confirmation that the bubble is breaking. He would hold the remaining large index puts through Nvidia's November 17 earnings and the December 9 Fed meeting. He would keep taking partial profits into sharp down days and add to cheap, cash-generating longs as tech falls. He is the clearest "short the AI index" voice among the legends.

### Bill Ackman

**Strategy and rules.** Ackman runs a concentrated book of 8–12 simple, predictable businesses that generate free cash flow, often pushing for change as an activist. He adds occasional asymmetric macro hedges: in 2020 he turned a **$27M CDS hedge into $2.6B** ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/15689746)). Pershing Square has returned **15.9% a year net since 2004, against 10.7% for the S&P 500**, including +20.9% NAV growth in 2025 ([PSH 2025 Annual Report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18175039/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2025-Annual-Report.pdf)).

**What he is doing in 2025–2026.** In 2025 he made Uber his top holding and took control of Howard Hughes ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4850529-bill-ackman-pershing-square-13f-portfolio-q3-2025-update)). He bought **Microsoft after its February dip**, built Amazon up and exited Alphabet ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/bill-ackmans-pershing-square-buys-115159065.html)). His Q2 13F added Netflix, Visa, Mastercard, S&P Global and Alcon, and he bought ICE after June 30 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/17/bill-ackmans-pershing-square-took-a-new-stake-in-n/)). He would treat the AI selloff as a chance to add to dominant platforms with real cash flow, such as Meta, Amazon and Microsoft, and to financial "toll roads" like the card networks, exchanges and ratings agencies. He would avoid capital-heavy AI-infrastructure plays. Given his inflation worries, the widening in CCC spreads and record private-credit defaults, he might also buy a cheap, asymmetric credit hedge like the 2020 CDS trade, though nothing reported shows he has done so.

## Macro traders mostly see a late-cycle boom and fear long bonds

### Ray Dalio

**Strategy and rules.** Dalio's framework combines long-term debt cycles, a "Changing World Order" model built on five forces (debt and money, internal conflict, external conflict, acts of nature, technology), and risk-balanced diversification through **All Weather**, a strategy launched in 1996 to perform across different economic environments ([LEADERS](https://www.leadersmag.com/issues/2025.3_JUL/PUR/LEADERS_Dalio_Bridgewater.html); [Ainslie Bullion](https://ainsliebullion.com.au/News-Resources/Article/Must-Read-Gold-The-Changing-World-Order/ID/2670)).

**What he is saying in 2025–2026.** In January 2026 he said "the monetary order is breaking down" and suggested **5–15% in gold** ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/billionaire-dalio-sends-2-word-warning-as-stocks-sell-off)). In June he said the AI bubble will burst when wealth is converted into money, triggered by rising rates and heavy stock issuance ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/dalio-sees-ai-bubble-bursting-as-wealth-is-converted-into-money)). In August he forecast a **US debt crisis within 1–5 years (about 3 as his best guess)** and advised investors to **sell or underweight bonds and hold 10–15% gold plus "a bit" of Bitcoin** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/21/ray-dalio-bessent-debt-crisis-bitcoin-gold.html)). On **October 7** he called AI "a classic bubble" that is "close" to bursting, said he prefers gold over Bitcoin, and warned that Bitcoin could fall with tech ([Yahoo SG](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/billionaire-ray-dalio-warns-ai-142955548.html)). Dalio no longer runs Bridgewater, so the firm's filings do not reflect his personal view.

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Both of the triggers Dalio named are now live: a Fed that is raising rates, and a 2027 pipeline of large IPOs including OpenAI and Anthropic. Gold has fallen to about $4,141, down 22–28% from its record ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold)), which on his thesis is a better entry point than January. He would be **long gold at the top of his 10–15% range**, hold a small Bitcoin sleeve, and stay underweight long-duration Treasuries and mega-cap AI. He would diversify across currencies and countries against "capital war" risk, all on top of a balanced All Weather core rather than a single concentrated bet.

### George Soros

**Strategy and rules.** Soros's theory of **reflexivity** holds that investors' biased beliefs change the fundamentals themselves. That produces self-reinforcing booms and busts: ride the boom, then bet heavily against it once the prevailing bias becomes unsustainable ([Morningstar India](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/48402/discount-obvious-bet-unexpected.aspx)). On Black Wednesday in 1992 his Quantum Fund shorted sterling for a profit of about $1B+ ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2010/08/23/2003481107)).

**What his firm is doing in 2025–2026.** Soros is 96, and Soros Fund Management is run by professionals. No 2025–2026 public commentary from Soros himself was found. SFM's Q2 2026 13F (about $8.1B; totals vary by tracker) is led by Amazon, TSMC, Global Payments, Alphabet and Nvidia. During the quarter it **trimmed Amazon, TSMC and Nvidia, added a new Nvidia put**, and bought utilities AEP and Entergy plus Nebius, Super Micro, and Micron, the last raised roughly sevenfold ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/mu-micron-stock-billionaire-george-soros-soros-fund-management-llc-buys-more-micron-in-q2-2026-13f-filing); [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/george-soros-portfolio/)). *Positions are as of June 30. SFM's currency, rates and short positions do not appear in the filing.*

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** The AI boom fits Soros's reflexive template closely. Rising valuations made it easy to raise debt and equity, which funded capex, which in turn produced the revenue that justified the valuations. The cracks are now visible: OpenAI's run rate reportedly came in at about $50B against the $68–70B assumed, CoreWeave bonds are near 15%, and the Fed is hiking ([StationX](https://app.stationx.net/articles/openai-finances-leak)). A Soros-style trader would turn decisively bearish on the most financing-dependent links in the chain, such as neoclouds and leveraged AI lenders, while keeping power and utility "picks and shovels" that benefit whatever happens. The filing hints at that shift: a Nvidia put plus trims to mega-caps.

### Stanley Druckenmiller

**Strategy and rules.** Druckenmiller trades liquidity and central-bank policy. He concentrates when conviction is high, a lesson he credits to Soros, who taught him to "be a pig" when he is right. He reverses quickly when he is wrong, and is reported to have gone about 30 years without a losing year ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/quotes/druckenmiller-quotes/); [Investing Time Daily](https://investingtimedaily.com/stanley-druckenmiller-portfolio/)).

**What he is doing in 2025–2026.** In early 2026 he said "AI is not driving the engine anymore." He was **bearish on the dollar** and expected Fed cuts ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/videos/hard-lessons/duquesne-stan-druckenmiller-iliana-bouzali)). By about September he had turned. He said rate cuts were unnecessary, Treasury yields were too low, and "we are likely in a profit bubble." He had cut AI holdings to **about 20% of their level six months earlier**. He was short the euro and pound in small size but "does not dare" short the dollar ([Investing.com/FT](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/druckenmiller-says-us-interest-rates-too-low-criticizes-fed--ft-93CH-4896700)). His Q2 2026 13F (about $5.2B) has **Natera as the top holding (about 17–22%)**. It shows exits from Broadcom, Intel and Micron, a more than tenfold increase in Amazon (stock plus calls), and a return to Alphabet ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/druckenmiller-dumps-micron-intel-buys-172050129.html); [TraceFour](https://tracefour.com/trackers/druckenmiller)). *Data caveat: this is June 30 data. Since he said in September that he had cut AI by about 80%, the Q3 filing will probably look different.*

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** The Fed under Warsh, whom he has praised, has come around to his view by raising rates in September and preparing to do so again. A liquidity trader reads that as tightening, which is not the time to load up on risk. He would stay **short or underweight long duration**, keep AI exposure small and focused on cash-generating platforms (Amazon, Alphabet) rather than semis and memory, and keep the small euro and pound shorts. His biggest bets would stay idiosyncratic, such as biotech. He would wait for the Fed to pivot before re-engaging aggressively with a washed-out Nvidia.

### Paul Tudor Jones

**Strategy and rules.** Jones puts defense first and lets price lead. His best-known rule is to get out of "anything that falls below the 200-day moving average." He never averages down on losing positions. He became famous for calling and profiting from the 1987 crash ([Value Fund Substack](https://valuefund.substack.com/p/paul-tudor-jones-200-day-moving-average); [T3 Live](https://www.t3live.com/?p=68073)).

**What he is saying in 2025–2026.** In April 2026 he called Bitcoin "unequivocally the best inflation hedge," ahead of gold, and said stocks are stretched ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/28/paul-tudor-jones-calls-bitcoin-the-best-inflation-hedge-warns-of-overvalued-stocks)). In **May** he said the market feels like 1999, the AI bull has "another year or two" with perhaps 40% more upside, and he was buying AI baskets ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/07/paul-tudor-jones-says-ai-bull-market-has-another-year-or-two-to-run.html)). By **October 13** he warned that **2027 could be "really, really challenging"** because of AI IPO supply and debt-market crowding ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/paul-tudor-jones-2027-really-challenging-year.html)).

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** His rule decides the trade. Our sources do not give 200-day levels. A 30% fall in Nvidia and an 11% one-day drop in SMH are the kind of breaks that would put leading AI names on the wrong side of his line and trigger exits. The broader index, still up about 10% for the year, may still be above trend, so he would keep only the AI exposure that is still trending up and tighten stops. He would get defensive ahead of 2027 supply. Hot October CPI and Bitcoin at about $74K, about 29% below a year ago, fit his inflation-hedge case for adding Bitcoin, alongside commodities and a bearish stance on long bonds ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/price-of-bitcoin-11-09-2026/)).

### Jesse Livermore

**Strategy and rules.** Livermore (1877–1940) bought breakouts through "pivotal points," the price levels where a stock's behavior changes. He added only to winning positions ("pyramiding"), cut losers fast, and held big trends, because "it never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting." He shorted 1929 only after the market confirmed the turn and reportedly netted about $100M. He also went broke several times ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/2023487); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Livermore)).

**What is happening now.** He has no modern positions, so his rules work only as a framework.

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Livermore would refuse to average down into broken AI leaders. He would rotate to stocks still making new highs on volume (27 S&P names hit 52-week highs on November 9, per [Trefis](https://trefis.com/stock/spy/articles/623917/the-52-week-high-list-27-sp-500-names-on-monday/2026-11-10)). He would treat energy's 2026 leadership, now unwinding with oil 22% off its peak, as a failed trend. He would go short the market only if the index confirmed a break, not on valuation alone.

## Quants, indexers and disruption bulls offer three very different answers

It holds only insiders' money. The funds open to outside investors have delivered ordinary, sometimes sharply negative, results ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies)). Simons died in 2024.

**What Renaissance is doing in 2025–2026.** The outside-investor fund RIEF fell **14.39% in October 2025** and was only about +2.3% for 2025 through November ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/renaissance-suffers-huge-losses-october); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/premium/renaissances-public-funds-stage-double-digit-comeback-november)). **Inference: view and likely trades today.** Simons would hold no opinion on the market as a whole. The swings in the 13F reflect model signals, not conviction. Renaissance's models would profit from 2026's volatility and the dispersion between AI winners and losers. Individuals cannot copy the strategy at all.

### John Bogle

**Strategy and rules.** Bogle, who founded Vanguard and died in 2019, argued that investors collectively earn the market's return minus costs, which makes **cost the only reliable predictor of net returns** ([IFA](https://www.ifa.com/articles/john_bogle_cost_matters_hypothesis)). His prescription: buy the whole haystack, hold roughly your age in bonds as a starting point, and stay the course ([myetfjourney](https://myetfjourney.com/blog/jack-bogle-philosophy)).

**What Vanguard is doing in 2025–2026.** Vanguard's 2026 outlook, "AI exuberance: Economic upside, stock market downside," called US valuations **"stretched."** It favored bonds, international stocks and US value ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/2026-outlook-economic-upside-stock-market-downside.html)). A secondary report of Vanguard's September 30 model puts US stocks at **4.8–6.8% a year over ten years, against 5.1–6.1% for US bonds**, with US equity valuations at the **99th percentile** ([EBC](https://www.ebc.com/forex/bonds-vs-stocks-return-forecasts)). That figure comes from one secondary source.

**Inference: view and likely trades today.** Bogle would tell investors to change almost nothing. Keep buying a total-market index fund at the lowest possible cost. Rebalance back to target, which after years of US gains means trimming stocks into bonds. Accept lower expected returns calmly instead of trading on AI headlines. With bonds now forecast to return about as much as stocks, a modestly larger bond allocation for older investors is consistent with his age rule. He was lukewarm on international stocks, though Vanguard now favors them.

### Cathie Wood

**Strategy and rules.** ARK holds concentrated, high-conviction positions in converging "disruptive innovation" platforms: AI, robotics, energy storage, blockchain, genomics and space. It sets five-year price targets and buys aggressively on dips ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ark-invest-releases-big-ideas-2026-a-global-signal-to-help-investors-own-whats-next-302667041.html)). ARK "went big on Nvidia" in September while selling AMD ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/etfs/broad-u-s-equity-etfs/26/09/62069468/cathie-wood-goes-big-on-nvidia-after-150-billion-buyback-sells-110-million-of-amd-ark-invest-also-picks-up-tesla-spacex-shares)), and it bought **CoreWeave** in the October selloff ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/19/were-still-in-the-parking-lot-cathie-wood-says-ai-isnt-a-bubble-heres-the-case-against-her/?tpid=1684094&tv=link&tc=in_content)). ## What an ordinary investor can actually use from all this

The most useful finding is where the legends agree, because their disagreements cancel out. Value investors, macro traders and Vanguard's model all say long-run US equity valuations are high and the extra return for owning stocks over bonds is thin. None of them has a reliable record of timing the top: PTJ said "another year or two" in May and Burry says "0–9 months." The sensible response is the one Buffett and Bogle share. Keep a low-cost, broadly diversified index core. Rebalance mechanically, which after several strong years means trimming stocks back to your target. Lower your return expectations instead of chasing or fleeing the AI trade. Concentration is a hidden risk in that core: an S&P 500 fund now puts about a third of your money in seven companies. Adding equal-weight, value or international funds (Japan is up 25% this year) diversifies away from the bet the legends argue about most.

The current environment also rewards patience in a concrete way. T-bills and money-market funds pay about 4%, and Vanguard's model forecasts bonds earning roughly what US stocks earn. Holding some short-term cash and high-grade bonds is not giving up: it is what Berkshire, Graham and Klarman call dry powder, and it lets you buy when others are forced to sell. The legends who warn about debt (Dalio, Druckenmiller, Marks and PTJ) agree on one thing: be careful with **long-dated** Treasuries while deficits run near $2T and the Fed is raising rates. That argues for short-to-intermediate bond funds over long ones. On gold and Bitcoin they split. Dalio favors gold, PTJ favors Bitcoin, and Buffett and Munger reject both. A small hedge position, if you hold one at all, is the reasonable middle ground.

Three cautions follow directly from the evidence. First, **do not copy 13F filings**. The latest available ones show June 30 positions, up to about 4.5 months old. They omit shorts, hedges and cash, and investors such as Druckenmiller say they have since cut those very positions sharply. Second, **avoid leverage and the "casino."** Buffett's warning about ultra-short-dated options, Dalio's example of a $100 AI stake bought on margin that falls to $25, and Livermore's repeated bankruptcies all make the same point. Third, **if you speculate, ring-fence it**, as Graham advised: keep a small, separate account with a written exit rule, such as PTJ's 200-day line or Livermore's rule of never adding to a loser, so that one bad bet cannot damage your core savings. Credit offering 15% yields, as on CoreWeave's bonds, and private-credit products with record defaults are for professionals like Marks and Klarman. They are not income substitutes for individuals.

## Conclusion

The striking pattern in November 2026 is not that the legends are bearish. It is that their caution is about **price and financing**, not about the technology. Marks, Dalio, Klarman and even Druckenmiller accept that AI is transformative. What worries them is debt-funded capex consuming most of cash flow, a $2T deficit competing for the same capital, and a Fed that is raising rates instead of cutting. That makes the next catalysts, Nvidia's November 17 earnings, the December 9 FOMC and the 2027 IPO wave, tests of whether financing holds up, not tests of AI's usefulness. A second, less obvious takeaway is that "Buffett-style" has become an institutional style. Abel's Berkshire turned over 16 positions in a quarter, bought airlines and a homebuilder, and repurchased stock at a record pace, all while keeping a third of a trillion dollars in T-bills. The lesson for individuals is that discipline is not inactivity. It is having a clear rule for what you will buy, at what price, and keeping the cash to do it when the price comes.
