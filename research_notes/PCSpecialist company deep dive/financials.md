## 2. Year-by-year financials (FY = year to 31 August)

### Takeaway
Turnover grew from about **£38m (FY2015) to a pandemic peak of about £146m (FY2021)**. It then fell to about **£116m in FY2023**, recovered to **£132m (FY2024)** and reached **£143m (FY2025)**, the second-highest on record. The business runs on very thin margins: operating margin was about 1% before 2019, peaked at about 3.1% in FY2021, and has been about 2.1–2.4% since. Net assets rose every year to **£23.2m (FY2025)**. There are no outstanding charges, which suggests little or no bank debt.

## 3. COVID-19 boom, post-boom normalisation and 2024–2026 developments

### Takeaway
On aggregator figures from the filings, the pandemic produced a **step-change**:
- Turnover rose about 55% in FY2020 (to £102.6m) and about 43% in FY2021 (to £146.4m), more than double FY2019 (£66.3m).
- Operating profit rose from £1.1m (FY2019) to £4.6m (FY2021).

The boom unwound over FY2022 (−6%) and FY2023 (−16%, turnover about £115.8m). The FY2023 fall coincided with the cost-of-living squeeze and the post-lockdown slump in PC demand. Recovery came in FY2024 (+14%) and FY2025 (+8.4%, £143m). No company-specific statements about 2025–26 DRAM/SSD inflation, GPU launches or Windows 10 end-of-support were found.

### Inferences
- FY2020 (September 2019 to August 2020) captured the first lockdown. FY2021 (September 2020 to August 2021) captured the second and third lockdowns plus GPU and crypto-era scarcity. FY2021 was the peak year for both turnover and profit.
- Operating margin roughly doubled from 1.6% (FY2019) to 3.1% (FY2021). The likely reasons are pricing power during component scarcity and fixed-cost leverage.
- After the boom, FY2023 turnover was down about 21% from peak, yet operating profit held at about £2.7m. The business protected profit better than turnover, though margins settled at about 2.1–2.4%, still well above pre-COVID levels of about 1%.
- The FY2024–FY2025 recovery coincides with the RTX 40-series Super refresh (January 2024), the RTX 50-series launch (January 2025) and the run-up to Windows 10 end-of-support (14 October 2025, inside FY2026). FY2025 ended in August 2025, so the bulk of the **DRAM/SSD price spike (late 2025–2026) and the Windows 10 deadline fall in FY2026** (year to 31 August 2026). Those accounts are not due until about May 2027, or 31 December 2026 if the year-end was shortened. **The impact of these events on PCSpecialist's financials is not yet visible in any filed accounts.**
- The large FY2025 rise in total assets and liabilities (section 2) may reflect stock-building or higher activity ahead of these events. Unverified.
