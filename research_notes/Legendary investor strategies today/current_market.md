> Big-picture context the report writer needs: the late-2026 backdrop is NOT the 2025 "Fed-cutting, Goldilocks" setup. In 2026 there is (1) a US–Iran war that sent Brent to $118 (Oct 30) and pushed inflation back up, (2) a Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh that **hiked** in September 2026 and is expected to hike again in December, (3) Democrats sweeping both chambers of Congress in the Nov 3 midterms, (4) a sharp AI-stock correction (Nvidia down ~30% from its Oct 6 record), and (5) gold down ~22–28% from its January 2026 record. Earnings, however, are growing more than 25% year over year, which has pushed the forward P/E *down* to a 3-year low even as CAPE stays near dot-com highs.

### Inferences
- **Equity risk premium (computed, not sourced):** forward earnings yield = 1/17.7 ≈ **5.65%** vs 10-yr Treasury **4.88%** (Nov 9) → ERP ≈ **0.8 percentage points**, thin by historical standards. A CAPE-based earnings yield (1/38.9 ≈ 2.6%) is about **2.3 pp *below*** the 10-yr yield, i.e., a negative "Fed model" ERP. Against a 10-yr TIPS yield (not found; see Gaps) it would look somewhat better.
- **Two valuation pictures:** Value investors (Buffett, Marks, Burry) will focus on CAPE about 39–41 and market cap/GDP about 220%+, readings comparable only to 1999–2000 and 2021. Growth-at-a-reasonable-price investors (Lynch) can point to a 17.7x forward P/E on >25% earnings growth. The key question is whether 2026's 33.6% forward-EPS jump is sustainable or a cyclical/AI-capex peak (energy profits from $90–118 oil are also inflating it).
- Mag 7 / top 10 concentration (about 35–38%) is well above the 2000 peak (about 27%). That favors equal-weight and "ex-mega-cap" ideas.

Burry-style bears will focus on circular financing, FCF turning negative at hyperscalers, and CoreWeave/Oracle credit. Lynch/GARP types may see the PEG ratio as compelling. Munger/Buffett-style investors would likely stress capital intensity: hyperscalers turning from asset-light compounders into capital-heavy utilities.
- The new Democratic Congress adds a policy risk (data-center permitting, power costs) that did not exist in 2025.

The Fed cut through 2025 to 3.50–3.75%, held through mid-2026, **hiked 25 bp on Sep 16, 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, held on Oct 28 (10–2, with two dissents *for a hike*), and markets price about 85–88% odds of another hike on Dec 9.
