## Ray Dalio (Bridgewater founder): All Weather, the debt cycle, the changing world order, and his 2025-2026 warnings

### Takeaway
Dalio's framework rests on three ideas: long-term debt cycles, the "changing world order" (five forces), and risk-balanced diversification (All Weather / risk parity). Through 2026 it produced a consistent call. He sees the US heading toward a debt crisis in roughly 1-5 years (best guess ~3), so he would underweight bonds, hold 10-15% in gold plus "a bit" of Bitcoin, and treat AI equities as a "classic bubble" nearing its pricking point. The triggers he names are rising rates, heavy equity issuance (IPOs), and forced conversion of wealth into cash. As of October 2026 he favors gold over Bitcoin.

### Cited Findings
**Core strategy and rules**
- Dalio's innovations are risk parity, alpha overlay and All Weather. All Weather launched in 1996 and was designed to perform across shifts in economic environments rather than bet on one — [LEADERS Magazine, Jul 2025](https://www.leadersmag.com/issues/2025.3_JUL/PUR/LEADERS_Dalio_Bridgewater.html); [Bridgewater bio](https://bridgewater.com/people/ray-dalio)
- He separates the short-term debt cycle (the business cycle, which he says the Fed and most economists model) from the long-term debt cycle. The long-term cycle is what drives today's warnings — [TradingKey analysis](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/commodities/metal/261764197-ray-dalio-debt-cycles-gold-all-weather-framework)
- His "Changing World Order" model rests on five interacting forces: the debt/money cycle, internal conflict, external conflict, acts of nature, and technology. Great conflicts or wars are followed by new orders — [Ainslie Bullion summary](https://ainsliebullion.com.au/News-Resources/Article/Must-Read-Gold-The-Changing-World-Order/ID/2670); [Pocket Casts transcript, "Five Forces"](https://pocketcasts.com/podcasts/c8a51a10-66d1-0133-d185-0d11918ab357/6d0f2889-8ca7-43c9-8782-1cb240506f16/transcript)
- He argues against holding cash in this environment and favors a well-diversified, non-cash portfolio such as All Weather, sized to the investor's risk tolerance — [Ainslie Bullion](https://ainsliebullion.com.au/News-Resources/Article/Must-Read-Gold-The-Changing-World-Order/ID/2670)
- "Principles" (book) documents his decision-making and management system — [Wikipedia: Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principles_(book))
- Mar 2025: he warned of a looming debt crisis and a shifting global order amid Trump's hardline policies — [Benzinga, Mar 2025](https://drupal-cloudfront.benzinga.com/markets/25/03/44511006/billionaire-investor-ray-dalio-warns-of-looming-debt-crisis-and-shifting-global-order-amid-trumps-hardlin)

## George Soros (Soros Fund Management): reflexivity, boom-bust, Black Wednesday, and recent 13F

### Takeaway
Soros's edge is reflexivity: market participants' biased beliefs change fundamentals, which creates self-reinforcing boom-bust sequences. You ride the boom and bet heavily on the bust once the prevailing bias becomes unsustainable. His signature trade was shorting sterling in 1992. Today SFM is a diversified family office (CIO Dawn Fitzpatrick; this is general knowledge, not sourced here) whose Q2 2026 13F is broad, about $8.1B across ~250+ names. It leans toward AI and mega-cap tech (Amazon, TSMC, Alphabet, Nvidia), was trimming those in Q2 2026, added a Nvidia put, and added utilities/power (AEP, Entergy) and AI infrastructure (Nebius, Super Micro, Micron).

### Cited Findings
**Core strategy**
- Reflexivity is Soros's philosophical investing synthesis. Awareness of it, he says, made him an astute investor. Key texts: *The Alchemy of Finance* — [TradingView summary (secondary)](https://www.tradingview.com/ideas/soros); [Morningstar India](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/48402/discount-obvious-bet-unexpected.aspx)

**Famous trade: Black Wednesday, Sep 16, 1992**
- Quantum Fund shorted the pound. Druckenmiller (CIO) generated the idea and Soros pushed him to take a "gigantic position". Profit was about $1B+ (one source says ~$1.1B). The fund ended 1992 up ~69%. The UK left the ERM after failed rate hikes. Position size estimates conflict, ranging from ~$1.5B early on to "$10B" — [Taipei Times, 2010](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2010/08/23/2003481107); [Jimmy's Journal](https://jimmysjournal.substack.com/p/go-big-or-go-home-lessons-from-soros); [Fortunly](https://fortunly.com/articles/george-soros-and-the-bank-of-england); [Wealthyhood](https://wealthyhood.com/uk/learning-guides/invest-like-george-soros/black-wednesday/)

## Paul Tudor Jones (Tudor Investment): trend following, the 200-day rule, the 1987 crash, inflation hedges, the 1999 analogy

### Takeaway
PTJ is a technically driven macro trader: defense first, exit anything below its 200-day moving average, focus on price over fundamentals. He became famous for calling and profiting from the 1987 crash. In 2026 he describes the market as "1999": in May he said the AI bull has "another year or two" and maybe 40% more upside, with a "breathtaking" correction to follow. By Oct 13, 2026 he warned that **2027 could be "really, really challenging"** because of big-tech IPO supply (Anthropic, OpenAI) and debt-market crowding. He calls Bitcoin "unequivocally the best inflation hedge", ahead of gold.

### Cited Findings
**Core strategy and rules**
- 200-day rule: "My metric for everything I look at is the 200-day moving average of closing prices... one principle for sure would get out of anything that falls below the 200-day moving average." He used it to minimize drawdowns in crashes. This is secondary quote collections; wording varies — [Value Fund Substack](https://valuefund.substack.com/p/paul-tudor-jones-200-day-moving-average); [TraderLion PTJ quotes](https://traderlion.com/quotes/paul-tudor-jones-quotes/); [25iq](https://25iq.com/2015/07/25/a-dozen-things-ive-learned-from-paul-tudor-jones-about-investing-and-trading)
- His trading rules emphasize defense over offense, cutting losers, never averaging losers, and that price moves first — [21 trading rules from Paul Tudor (Substack)](https://wallstreettrader.substack.com/p/21-trading-rules-from-paul-tudor); [T3 Live](https://www.t3live.com/?p=68073)
- 1987 crash: he called it and profited, in documentary footage ("Trader", 1987) — [T3 Live](https://www.t3live.com/?p=68073); [Dailymotion, Lessons of Trading](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x5vrdmu)

He has also made money in gold historically — [CoinDesk, Apr 28 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/28/paul-tudor-jones-calls-bitcoin-the-best-inflation-hedge-warns-of-overvalued-stocks); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/people-culture/paul-tudor-jones-sees-bitcoin-as-best-inflation-hedge/)

## Jesse Livermore (historical, 1877-1940): tape reading, pivotal points, pyramiding, cutting losses, "the sitting"

### Takeaway
Livermore traded price action. He bought breakouts through "pivotal points", added only to winners (pyramiding), cut losers fast, and held big winners through the trend, because "it never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting." His most famous trades were short sales into the 1907 panic and the 1929 crash, the latter reportedly netting ~$100M (figures vary).

### Cited Findings
- Pivotal points are price levels where a stock's or market's character changes. He bought breakouts above them and sold breaks below — [Moomoo, Trade Like Jesse Livermore](https://www.moomoo.com/us/learn/trade-like-jesse-livermore); [IG](https://ig.com/en-ch/trading-strategies/the-most-successful-traders-of-all-time-40343-171023)
- Pyramiding rule: add to positions that show a profit, never to positions that show a loss — [Benzinga, 1929 Crash and the $100M Profit](https://benzinga.com/z/2023487)
- "It never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It always was my sitting." Also paraphrased as "the big money is made by the sittin' and the waitin'". From *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefèvre, 1923) — [Kitco, May 2 2023](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2023-05-02/jesse-livermore-worlds-greatest-trader); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Livermore)
- 1929: he built huge short positions in early 1929 through 100+ brokers to hide his activity, was down over $6M on paper by spring, and netted ~$100M in the crash. The exact figure is not well documented — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Livermore); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/2023487); [Topstep](https://e.topstep.com/7-greatest-trades-jesse-livermore)
- He went broke several times and died by suicide in 1940 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Livermore)

### Inferences
- Applied to Nov 2026, Livermore rules point to staying with leaders (AI megacaps) only while they keep making new highs on volume, and selling or reversing on a failed breakout or a break of the pivotal point. No averaging down into a broken AI trade. His 1929 template (short only after the market confirms the turn, not on valuation alone) echoes PTJ's "1999, but the top comes later" stance.
- No modern positions apply. Use him as a rules framework only.

### Gaps
- Most Livermore material available here is secondary trading-education content. *Reminiscences* and *How to Trade in Stocks* (1940) were not consulted directly.
