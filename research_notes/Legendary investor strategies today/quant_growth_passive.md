## Jim Simons / Renaissance Technologies (Medallion, RIEF/RIDA)

### Takeaway
Medallion is a closed, very high-turnover statistical-arbitrage machine. Zuckerman puts its arithmetic average return at about 66% gross and 39% net from 1988 to 2018. Individuals can't copy it, and neither can Renaissance's own outside-investor funds (RIEF/RIDA), which have produced ordinary and volatile results. Those funds had a whipsaw 2025 (+22.7% in 2024; −14% in Oct 2025; roughly +2% YTD by end-Nov 2025) and a weak start to 2026. The Q2 2026 13F shows a huge, diversified book of about $72.6B across about 3,140 names, led by mega-cap AI/tech (NVIDIA, Meta, Alphabet, Palantir), with Apple and Intel trimmed.

- Medallion fee structure is widely reported as 5% management plus 44% performance (post-2002); some sources cite a 20–40% performance-fee range for earlier years — [the7circles / Medallion summaries via search](https://the7circles.uk/?p=35029); [Of Dollars and Data](https://ofdollarsanddata.com/medallion-fund/)

### Inferences
- Why it can't be copied: Medallion depends on very short holding periods, thousands of weak signals, heavy leverage, execution infrastructure and a capped capacity (it returns profits to stay small), none of which an individual has. The 13F book is a byproduct of model-driven long/short institutional strategies, not a stock-picking "view" (positions are each under 2%, quarterly turnover is high, and NVIDIA swung from $1.05B to $0.16B to $1.4B within three quarters).
- "Simons today" would not express a macro view. He would trade short-horizon dislocations, which 2026's AI-led volatility and dispersion should provide. The sharp Q4 2025–Q2 2026 swing back into AI megacaps reads as a momentum/factor signal, not conviction.

## John Bogle / Vanguard (index investing, current outlook)

### Takeaway
Bogle's doctrine is to own the whole market at the lowest cost, hold bonds roughly in line with your age, and stay the course. Costs are the one reliable predictor of net returns (his "Cost Matters Hypothesis"). Vanguard's current house view in 2025–2026 keeps a valuation-driven caution on US stocks, especially US growth. Its forecasts are higher for non-US stocks, US value and bonds, and as of late 2026 its model puts US equity valuations near the top of their historical range (99th percentile, per a secondary source).

### Cited Findings
**Core strategy and rules**
- Cost Matters Hypothesis: formalized in Bogle's 2005 *Financial Analysts Journal* article "The Relentless Rules of Humble Arithmetic". In it, investors' net return equals the gross market return minus the costs of financial intermediation, framed as a counterpart to the EMH that "does not require us to believe that every active manager lacks skill" — [IFA: Bogle Cost Matters Hypothesis](https://www.ifa.com/articles/john_bogle_cost_matters_hypothesis)
- "Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack" is widely attributed to Bogle, but sources vary in wording and treat it as a commonly attributed saying — [pulse.bot summary](https://www.pulse.bot/personal-finance/news/own-the-haystack-not-the-needle-jack-bogles-investing-rules-everyone-over-50-should-follow-357e4826-a156-47d2-b572-5d04a260649a/); [Validea](https://blog.validea.com/?p=21302)
- Age-based allocation: bond percentage roughly equal to your age, as a starting point. Secondary sources only; others cite a "120 minus age" variant for stocks — [myetfjourney: Jack Bogle philosophy](https://myetfjourney.com/blog/jack-bogle-philosophy); [freedomisntfree](https://freedomisntfree.co.uk/articles/bogleheads)
- Staying the course: "the secret to investing is there is no secret"; avoid panic selling and timing. *Stay the Course: The Story of Vanguard and the Index Revolution* (2018) — [antoinebuteau: Lessons from John Bogle](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-john-bogle/); [SCU library record](https://libcat.scu.edu/Record/b3207991/TOC)
- Vanguard's index funds turned 50 in 2026, and "cost still matters" remains the framing — [DepositAccounts](https://www.depositaccounts.com/community/promos/62730-vanguard-funds-50-cost-matters.html)

## Philip Fisher (scuttlebutt, growth investing)

### Takeaway
Fisher's approach, from *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits* (1958), is qualitative and growth-focused. It rests on deep field research ("scuttlebutt") into a small number of outstanding companies, judged against a 15-point checklist that stresses durable sales growth, margins, R&D and management quality and integrity. The holding period is effectively forever ("the time to sell… almost never"), shown by Motorola, held from 1955 until his death in 2004. He influenced Buffett's shift from Graham-style cigar butts toward quality compounders; John Train's widely repeated framing calls Buffett 85% Graham and 15% Fisher.

### Cited Findings
- Scuttlebutt: gather "grapevine" information about a company from competitors, suppliers, customers, ex-employees and industry experts — [Wikipedia: Philip Arthur Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Arthur_Fisher); [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/investment-strategy/the-timeless-investment-wisdom-of-philip-fisher)
- The 15 points are a qualitative checklist and his most enduring contribution. Examples: products and markets large enough for years of above-average sales growth; management's determination to keep developing new products; R&D effectiveness; worthwhile profit margins and actions to maintain or improve them; labor and executive relations; depth of management; cost and accounting controls; long-range outlook on profits; avoiding dilution; candor in bad times; and point 15, management "of unquestionable integrity" (the non-negotiable) — [Novel Investor: Fisher's 15 Points](https://novelinvestor.com/philip-fishers-15-points/); [InvestmentNews review](https://www.investmentnews.com/guides/common-stocks-and-uncommon-profits-book-review/265548); [Old School Value checklist](https://www.oldschoolvalue.com/?p=15582)
- Selling rule: "If the job has been correctly done when a common stock is purchased, the time to sell it is — almost never." Valid reasons to sell are a mistake in the original analysis, deterioration of management, or loss of the ability to grow markets as before. Don't sell on economic or market forecasts — [moomoo: Investment Strategy of Philip Fisher](https://www.moomoo.com/us/learn/the-investment-strategy-of-philip-fisher); [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/philip-fishers-15-points/)
- Motorola: bought in 1955 when it was a radio maker and held until his death in 2004. One source says 1977, but 1955 is better supported. ### Inferences
- Fisher in today's market would ignore macro and AI-bubble debates and run scuttlebutt on AI-supply-chain and platform companies. He would ask customers, suppliers and competitors which firms have durable R&D leadership, margin power and candid, high-integrity management, and then concentrate in a few and hold for decades. He would differ from Wood mainly in his insistence on proven margins and management quality over TAM projections, and from Bogle in rejecting diversification for its own sake.
- His "almost never sell" rule implies tolerating large drawdowns in true compounders (the Motorola example), which matches Buffett's later Apple-style holding behavior.
