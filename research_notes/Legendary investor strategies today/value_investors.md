## Benjamin Graham (1894–1976): Mr. Market, margin of safety, net-nets, defensive vs. enterprising, 25–75% rule, Graham Number, speculation

### Takeaway
Graham's system is quantitative and balance-sheet-anchored: buy only with a margin of safety below conservatively computed value, treat market prices as an emotional counterparty ("Mr. Market") to exploit rather than obey, and keep a stock/bond mix between 25% and 75% equities, leaning toward bonds when stocks are expensive. Applied to a market at ~22x earnings dominated by AI megacaps (see Lynch/Marks/Klarman sections), Graham would be at or near the 25% equity floor for the defensive investor and would hunt for net-nets/low-P/B stocks outside the index leaders.

### Cited Findings
- **Graham Number** = √(22.5 × EPS × book value per share); it is presented as the theoretical maximum price a defensive investor should pay. The 22.5 constant comes from the Chapter 14 defensive criteria of *The Intelligent Investor* (P/E ≤ 15 × P/B ≤ 1.5 = 22.5). Attribution is disputed: some sources say Graham never proposed the formula directly, only the criteria it is derived from — [Wikipedia: Graham number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_number); [GuruFocus: Using the Graham Number correctly](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/197280/using-the-graham-number-correctly)
- **25–75% rule:** the defensive investor should never hold less than 25% or more than 75% of the portfolio in common stocks, with bonds the inverse; 50/50 is the default, shifting toward bonds when stocks look overvalued and toward stocks when they look cheap — [The Intelligent Investor: General Portfolio Policy, the Defensive Investor (summary)](https://intelligentinvestor-internetbusiness.blogspot.com/2009/11/general-portfolio-policy-defensive.html)
- **Defensive vs. enterprising investor:** *The Intelligent Investor* splits readers into the Defensive Investor (Ch. 4, 5, 14: diversified, large, financially strong, consistent dividend payers bought at moderate multiples) and the Enterprising Investor (Ch. 6, 7, 15: willing to do more work for bargain issues, special situations) — [Graham's Two-Investor Framework](https://glenbradfordcom.vercel.app/defensive-vs-enterprising-investor)
- **Net-nets:** an enterprising-investor bargain strategy: buy stocks trading below net current asset value (current assets minus all liabilities); net-net working capital variants haircut balance-sheet items (e.g., counting only 75% of receivables) — [Graham's Two-Investor Framework](https://glenbradfordcom.vercel.app/defensive-vs-enterprising-investor); [Benjamin Graham value investing principles](https://edison-dividends-com.pages.dev/articles/financial-analysis/benjamin-graham-value-investing-principles). [book] Graham's classic rule of thumb was buying below two-thirds of NCAV in a diversified basket.
- **Mr. Market / margin of safety / speculation** [book: *The Intelligent Investor*, Ch. 1, 8, 20]: Mr. Market is a manic-depressive business partner who offers daily prices; the investor should use his offers, never be guided by them. "Margin of safety" is named the central concept of investment (Ch. 20). Graham defined investment as an operation that, "upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return"; anything else is speculation. He did not ban speculation but insisted it be recognized as such and kept to a small, separate fund. (Not web-verified this session; standard text.)

### Inferences
- Graham's defensive criteria (P/E ≤ 15, P/B ≤ 1.5, long dividend record, earnings stability) would exclude nearly all AI megacaps today; a Graham screen in late 2026 would surface low-multiple financials, insurers, industrial/energy cyclicals, small caps and possibly non-U.S. (e.g., Japan) stocks.
- With U.S. 10-year yields elevated (Marks's Sept 2026 memo concerns long-dated government bond yields) and equities richly valued, the 25–75 rule implies a tilt toward the bond end (closer to 25–40% equities) for a defensive Graham follower.
- He would label much of the AI/momentum trade "speculation" — acceptable only in a ring-fenced allocation.

## Peter Lynch: invest in what you know, six categories, PEG, tenbagger, Magellan record, recent interviews

### Takeaway
Lynch is a bottom-up GARP (growth at a reasonable price) investor who classifies every stock into one of six categories, prices growth with the PEG ratio, and looks for tenbaggers among businesses he can understand. In his October 2025 interviews he said he owns no AI stocks, couldn't pronounce Nvidia until recently, and declined to call a bubble, while noting the market trades ~22x earnings and that even Costco carries an "AI stock multiple."

### Cited Findings
- **Magellan record:** ~29.2% annualized from 1977 to 1990, nearly twice the S&P 500's ~15.8% over the same period; assets grew from ~$18 million to ~$14 billion — [Validea: How Peter Lynch doubled the market at Magellan](https://blog.validea.com/?p=13923); [MarketScreener: Peter Lynch's career](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/latest/Peter-Lynch-s-investment-philosophy-45510980)
- **Six categories:** slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, asset plays, turnarounds — [Validea: The Investment Strategy of Peter Lynch](https://blog.validea.com/?p=32501); [Validea: The Lynch Approach: GARP, PEGs and Profits](https://blog.validea.com/?p=7627)
- **PEG ratio** = P/E ÷ expected earnings growth rate; ~1.0 is fair, below 1.0 suggests undervaluation; Lynch added dividend yield to growth for slow growers/stalwarts (dividend-adjusted PEG) — [Validea: The Lynch Approach](https://blog.validea.com/?p=7627)
- **Tenbagger:** term Lynch coined for a stock that rises 10x; candidates typically grow earnings 15–25% a year at PEG near or below 1.0 (one German source uses PEG < 1.5; thresholds vary by source) — [Validea](https://blog.validea.com/?p=7627); [ETF-Nachrichten: Tenbagger finden](https://www.etf-nachrichten.de/news/tenbagger-finden-das-sind-die-peter-lynch-kriterien/)
- **"Invest in what you know" / know what you own:** his core message in recent interviews remained to understand what you own rather than predict the market — [Yahoo Finance, Oct 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/legendary-fund-manager-surprising-ai-173300284.html)
- **Oct 6, 2025 (CNBC / The Compound and Friends):** Lynch said he holds no AI stocks, "I literally couldn't pronounce Nvidia until about 8 months ago," and when asked whether the AI trade had gone too far said he had "no idea"; he said he doesn't understand the technology well enough to have an informed view — [CNBC, Oct 6, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/06/peter-lynch-ai-trade-investing.html)
- Asked by Josh Brown whether today's AI market echoes 1999: "I have no idea, I have a lot of stocks I like" — [X post summarizing interview, Oct 2025](https://x.com/GabGrowth/status/1974440450862801244)
- He pointed to the broader market at ~22x earnings and said Costco carries an "AI stock multiple" despite selling paper towels (secondhand newsletter recap) — [Compound with Rene](https://www.compoundwithrene.com/p/free-7-timeless-takeaways-from-peter)
- He reassured workers worried AI would take their jobs — [Benzinga, Nov 2025](https://www.benzinga.com/personal-finance/financial-advisors/25/11/48889460/peter-lynch-on-ai-stocks-i-literally-couldnt-pronounce-nvidia-until-about-eight-months-ago)

### Inferences
- Lynch would not macro-call the AI bubble; he would stay away from AI names he can't explain and fish in understandable mid-cap growers, stalwarts at reasonable PEGs, and cyclicals/turnarounds where the story is visible to a consumer. He would avoid "AI multiple" stalwarts like Costco on price.
- His framework would classify Nvidia as a cyclical (semiconductors) rather than a fast grower — implying caution about buying at peak earnings with a low trailing P/E.

### Gaps
- No 2026 Lynch interview found; his most recent public views are from October–November 2025.
- No disclosed personal portfolio (he holds no public 13F).

## Howard Marks (Oaktree): cycles, second-level thinking, risk control, the pendulum, credit; 2025–2026 memos and current stance

### Takeaway
Marks's framework — second-level thinking, cycle positioning, risk control, and the psychological "pendulum" — leads him in 2025–2026 to a calibrated "moderate, selective, prudent" stance: AI is likely an "inflection bubble" (transformative technology with excess capital), not a reason to go all-in or all-out; private credit/direct lending is facing its first real test; and as of Nov 2, 2026 he warns "there is no reward in the investment business for hyperactivity."

### Cited Findings
- **Core philosophy** [book: *The Most Important Thing*, 2011; *Mastering the Market Cycle*, 2018]: second-level thinking (being different and better than consensus: "what's priced in?"); risk means the probability of permanent loss, not volatility; "you can't predict, you can prepare"; investor psychology swings like a pendulum between greed and fear, and the best opportunities come at the extremes; know where you are in the cycle. (Book-based, not web-verified this session.)
- **"Is It a Bubble?" (late 2025; Oaktree tweet dated ~Dec 9, 2025):** distinguishes "mean-reversion" bubbles (e.g., 2008 subprime) from "inflection bubbles" that accompany revolutionary technology (railroads, electricity, 1990s broadband) where over-optimism funds infrastructure; says AI fits the latter; notes 1998–2000 P/Es of Microsoft/Cisco/Oracle were much higher than today's AI leaders; flags circular deals, aggressive use of debt in a technologically uncertain sector, and a possible data-center glut; advice: prudence and selectivity, and no one should go all-in without accepting the risk of ruin — [Oaktree memo page](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/is-it-a-bubble); [Oaktree on X](https://x.com/Oaktree/status/1998430811159409007); [Acquirer's Multiple, Dec 2025](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2025/12/howard-marks-how-to-invest-in-ai-without-getting-destroyed-by-the-bubble/); [Evergreen Gavekal](https://evergreengavekal.com/blog/is-it-a-bubble/)
- **Nov 12, 2025:** Marks highlighted credit "carelessness" but said the issues are not systemic — [CNBC, Nov 12, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/12/veteran-investor-howard-marks-warns-over-credit-carelessness.html)
- **"AI Hurtles Ahead" (Feb 26, 2026):** addendum to "Is It a Bubble?"; discusses how AI differs from prior innovations; recommends "a moderate position, applied with selectivity and prudence" — [Oaktree memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/ai-hurtles-ahead); [Acquirer's Multiple, Mar 2026](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2026/03/howard-marks-ai-hurtles-ahead-market-implications-for-long-term-investors/)
- **Oaktree Conference 2026 reflections (podcast, Mar 24, 2026):** evolution of credit from high yield to private credit; shift to multi-asset portfolio solutions — [Memo podcast listing](https://index.fame.so/show/the-memo-by-howard-marks/reflections-on-oaktree-conference-2026-with-howard-marks)
- **"What's Going on in Private Credit?" (Apr 9, 2026):** outlines growing concerns about private credit, especially direct lending, which faces its first major test since the GFC; explains reasons for its rapid growth and issues created by fast capital deployment — [Seeking Alpha news](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4573849-howard-marks-outlines-growing-concerns-about-private-credit-in-new-memo); [Brookfield Private Wealth repost](https://privatewealth.brookfield.com/insight/memos-howard-marks-whats-going-private-credit); [Acquirer's Multiple, Apr 2026](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2026/04/howard-marks-warns-private-credit-faces-a-crucial-test-for-investors/)
- **"Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics? – Part III" (Sep 22, 2026):** U.S. fiscal picture; argues federal deficits, ordinary capital needs and AI investment together create heavy demand for debt and equity capital; discusses attempts to rein in long-dated government bond yields — [Oaktree memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/shall-we-repeal-the-laws-of-economics---part-iii); [Memo podcast](https://music.amazon.com/podcasts/6066508f-70ef-4a94-8573-8454c8164734/the-memo-by-howard-marks)
- **Nov 2, 2026 (Bloomberg TV, The China Show):** "There is no reward in the investment business for hyperactivity," applied to AI stock trading — [24/7 Wall St., Nov 4, 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/04/billionaire-howard-marks-has-a-warning-for-ai-investors-theres-no-reward-for-rushing-in)

### Inferences
- Marks's pendulum reading in 2026 is "elevated but not euphoric-everywhere": he concedes bubble-like features in AI while refusing a binary call. His actionable bias is toward credit where yields are high but underwriting matters (he favors performing/opportunistic credit and is skeptical of the direct-lending crowd), and toward patience/dry powder for distressed opportunities if AI-capex debt or private credit cracks.
- His Sept 2026 fiscal memo implies concern about duration risk in long Treasuries and crowding-out from AI + deficit borrowing — consistent with preferring shorter-duration, high-yield-type credit over long government bonds.
- What he'd "buy": senior/secured credit and opportunistic debt at double-digit yields, distressed paper from over-levered AI infrastructure or software borrowers if dislocated; modest, selective equity exposure rather than broad AI beta.

## Seth Klarman (Baupost): Margin of Safety, holding cash, absolute returns; recent views and 13F positions

### Takeaway
Klarman is an absolute-return, risk-averse value investor who holds cash when bargains are scarce and buys from forced/uneconomic sellers. In 2026 he described the market as having "characteristics of a bubble" but is neither hiding in cash nor chasing AI; Baupost's ~$5.4B U.S. equity book (Q2 2026) is led by Amazon, Elevance Health and Restaurant Brands, with new stakes in CME Group and Pershing Square, ~10% exposure to AI beneficiaries at defensible multiples, plus raw land near power infrastructure and distressed software credits.

### Cited Findings
- **Core philosophy** [book: *Margin of Safety*, 1991]: avoid permanent loss before seeking gain; target absolute rather than relative returns; value is a range, so buy at a large discount; hold cash when nothing meets the bar; best bargains come from forced selling, complexity, spin-offs, bankruptcies and other institutional constraints; criticizes "relative performance" money managers. (Book-based, not web-verified this session.)
- **Global Alts New York 2026:** called the market as having "characteristics of a bubble"; Baupost avoids both retreating to cash and chasing the AI trade; ~10% of the book in companies benefiting from the AI rollout but only at multiples he can defend; holds raw land near power infrastructure as an optionality bet (no plan to build); among AI "losers," looks at software-related credits at very low cash-flow multiples; also cited assisted living — [iConnections, 2026](https://iconnections.io/insights/seth-klarman-baupost-ai-era-global-alts-ny-2026/)
- **On cash (2026):** "I'm not getting paid to hold cash, I'm getting paid to use my judgment on when to deploy the money"; Baupost now holds larger market-cap positions that let it pivot quickly, so it needs less cash — [iConnections, 2026](https://iconnections.io/insights/seth-klarman-baupost-ai-era-global-alts-ny-2026/)
- **June 2026, Masters in Business (Barry Ritholtz):** argued the big names carrying a highly concentrated market are often expensive; bottom-up investing beats forecasting — [Ritholtz transcript, Jun 2026](https://ritholtz.com/2026/06/transcript-seth-klarman/); [Acquirer's Multiple, Jun 2026](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2026/06/seth-klarman-why-bottom-up-investing-still-beats-market-forecasting/).
