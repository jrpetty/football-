# [redstone] The hopper sorter beside the storehouse: ten filter slices, each sorting one good into its own chest.
#
# The couriers empty their packs into the delivery chest at the left-hand end. A line of hoppers carries
# everything along to the right, under a slab roof. Under each hopper of the line sits a filter hopper
# holding twenty-two of its good (eighteen in its first slot and one in each of the others), so it will
# take nothing else from the line, and it gives nothing up of itself: it faces into its comparator. The
# comparator reads it at strength 1; the twenty-third it pulls from the line makes it 2. The signal runs
# back over two redstone dusts, so only a strength of 2 reaches the block under the second. That turns
# off the redstone torch on the block's back. The torch, while it burns, sends its power down and forward
# along the redstone underneath to a repeater, and the repeater powers the block under the output hopper,
# which keeps the output hopper locked. So: one of the good too many, and the lock lifts; the output
# hopper takes from the filter (always its first slot) and drops it into the good's own chest; the filter
# falls back to twenty-two, the torch lights, and the hopper is locked again. Whatever no filter wants goes
# on to the end of the line, into the overflow (in a town, a hopper there carries it on into the storehouse).
#
# The repeater is set to its longest delay on purpose. A torch put out and lit again for every item of a
# steady stream burns out (the game's own rule: eight times in three seconds), and a burnt-out torch let a
# filter run down to five. With the delay the lock stays lifted all through a stream, and after one lone
# item the output hopper takes one or two before it shuts: the torch is put out a few times a minute at
# most, and the filter never falls below twenty.
#
# The spare column between two slices is solid, so no slice's redstone touches its neighbour's; the glass
# over the back keeps feet off the torch and the dust.
