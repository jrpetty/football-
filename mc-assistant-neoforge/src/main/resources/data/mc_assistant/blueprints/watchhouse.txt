# [police] The watch house, the town's police station (from the Stone Age, once the town keeps a watch of three:
# WatchHouse): a squat house of the watch's own, all of stone and brick, with a lantern on a post either side of its
# door. Inside the door the front room: the records shelf down the left wall, where the watch keeps its case files,
# and on the right the front desk, a counter of barrels with the casebook open on a lectern behind it, where the
# constable sits and folk come to tell what they saw; the notice board of the wanted (wall signs, put up by the
# watch: WatchHouse) on the wall beside it. Across the back, behind a passage, two cells, each with a bed along its
# outer wall. Their fronts are drawn as fence posts either side of a wooden door: the watch sets iron bars in the
# posts' place and hangs an iron door in each, of the town's own iron (WatchHouse), once it can spare it. The
# lanterns hang flush under the stone ceiling, one in each cell, one over the passage and one over the front room.
# Rows run from the back of the building (top) to the front (bottom, the door).
